Post 01 · On Simplicity
The Houdini Solution: thinking inside the box
Reading along, "keep the ads simplified... choose the simplest way to write, the most creative images, the simplest ad films"...and suddenly came across a new concept: the "Houdini" solution. Put creativity and innovation to work by thinking inside the box. All this time I thought you had to "think out of the box" to come up with creative, unique solutions. But the Houdini theory points toward focus, simplicity, and speed within a specific limit of space and resources. This reminds me of something I hear all the time: "I can not make a video myself, I do not have the resources, I do not have a pro camera." So the Houdini Solution might be showing up at exactly the right time. All you need is a phone that can record, and a one of a kind idea. Do you remember the short videos that stayed with you longest? I still remember Vinamilk's "we are happy cows" jingle, and "Charlie bit my finger." Sixteen years later, still so natural, so moving. They did not need a professional camera at all. So the thing to do right now is: think and create an idea that lives inside the "box" to make an outstanding ad! Inspired by "Hey, Whipple, Squeeze This" (Luke Sullivan, Sam Bennett).
Post 02 · On Visual Storytelling
The eye remembers what the ear forgets
Whenever we sell something, we have to find some way to communicate with the customer. Telling the story of a product or a brand through images almost always works. People say the eye remembers what the ear forgets. So images that flow into images, what we call a short film, stir emotional memory even more. So if you are writing a short video script, think about a concept strong enough in visuals that a viewer could understand it even with the sound off. Watch the wordless film below. Post 03 · On Rhetorical Questions
Good headlines for March 8th ads
Good headlines for March 8th ads. If today is the day you are starting an ad campaign for it, congratulations. You are now part of the idea behind a rather sweet day. For as long as anyone remembers, women have spent their time caring for family and children, rarely setting time aside for themselves. Maybe close to March 8th, they do not even know what they should do that day. But if today we start with a few questions, maybe they will realize: tomorrow is March 8th. So how do you write a question that becomes a true rhetorical question. I would bet ChatGPT has never once produced a proper rhetorical question: Will tomorrow's outing be fun? Where does the real happiness of women on March 8th actually come from? What color does she really like? (I love a rhetorical question just like this one, from a famous flower ad: "exactly how angry is she?") Take your time writing it. Do I still love this craft of writing?
Post 04 · Relaunching the Group
Why this group exists
Today the admin is officially picking up the pen again (haha, well, typing on a phone) to reactivate a sweet little group called "Viết Ads," which I, the admin, left wide open for almost three years. Because I am someone obsessed with reading about advertising, analyzing ad content, in love with advertising language. I just wanted a place where like minded people could gather, talk about the craft of writing, and feel something from a piece of writing that moves us. Then I asked myself: how do words actually affect emotion. When I can still read, still feel something from words, it means my soul still has a corner of calm feeling that is not driven from the outside, but generates itself from within. Like me. After three years away from this group, I went back and read everything I had written. My first reaction was shock: "wait, did I really write this well back then." Then: "ugh, that sentence break is off, let me edit this." So in short, this Viết Ads group is a space to write, for anyone who wants to write, and a space to read, for anyone who wants to read. Writing ads is a craft, and a craft needs practice. So just write, just create. The words will keep you with yourself a little longer. SG15June2026. The day Lynn started writing again.
Post 05 · #moingaymottipcontent
Kindness in the way we write ads
When someone cares about branding, then from the moment they start shaping a concept for a new product or a new promotion, they need to think about "kindness." Kindness in writing depends on who the writer's heart is really directed toward: the audience, the brand, the words themselves, or the writer. Kindness shows up in word choice. Do not exaggerate the truth, do not dress up the language. Write in the simplest, closest way possible. Here is an example about an "antique style clock." See which version feels more "kind": 1. Time is more precious than gold or jewels. So the "Timeless Clock" was crafted from the rarest wood, by the most skilled artisans, to preserve time and connect past, present, and future. 2. Look at this clock on your wall, somewhere in your own private corner. A few beautiful memories will come rushing back. "Timeless Clock" keeps time. Now try another product: a newly launched perfume. 1. Every drop is the crystallization of hundreds of rare fragrance ingredients, delivering an unprecedented olfactory experience that confirms the status of its owner. 2. One day you spray a scent onto your shirt. That evening, you meet someone. They say, "Being near you makes me want to close my eyes and sleep peacefully." Guess who that person is, and what happens next. Version one tries to convince through grandeur. Version two simply tells one small, true moment. There is a scientific reason for this. Smell is the only sense with a direct pathway to the amygdala and hippocampus, the brain's centers for emotion and memory, bypassing the thalamus like other senses do not. That is why a scent can awaken a feeling almost instantly, before you even realize where it came from. So when writing about perfume, do not describe the scent. Describe the moment the reader's brain automatically remembers someone. That is real kindness toward the reader: it does not force belief, it simply awakens a feeling already inside them.
Post 06 · Teacher's Day Card
When gratitude finds its voice
Everyone in life owes gratitude to someone, at least once, a few times, or many times over. Today let us talk about "the debt we owe our teachers." A teacher is the one who gives us knowledge. But that word is so much bigger than it sounds. It carries inspiration, skill, and the simple act of living in a way that makes us admire them, that turns us toward goodness, that makes us better. Every mark on this card is made by hand, strand by strand of trúc chỉ paper woven together, the same quiet way a teacher weaves themselves into our life, one small thread at a time. A card that feels just right for this moment, when the gratitude in your heart wants to speak to your teacher.
