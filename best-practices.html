Field Notes

What actually works on Facebook in Vietnam.

Most brands write content like they're filling a form. Audiences in Vietnam don't respond to forms. They respond to people. Here are five things I've learned from running content that grew from 2,379 to 76,946 reach, and from training AI to evaluate what "good content" even means.

Years Inside Meta's Ecosystem
01
Principle
People don't share new things. They share things that remind them of home.

Nostalgia beats novelty

People don't share things because they're new. They share things because it reminds them of home. "Về Đà Nẵng nhớ mua Chả Bò" worked because it wasn't selling a product, it was selling a memory.

02
Principle
Write like a text from a friend, not a brand statement.

Short and real beats polished

Gen Z scrolls past anything that looks like an ad. The captions that performed best were the ones that sounded like a text from a friend, not a brand statement.

03
Principle
Write captions that invite a reply, not just a like.

The comment section is the second headline

On Meta, what happens in the comments often outperforms the caption itself. Write captions that invite a reply, not just a like.

04
Principle
Pick one feeling. Pride. Hunger. Nostalgia. Humor. Just one.

One emotion, not five messages

The best performing posts had one clear feeling: pride, hunger, nostalgia, humor. Trying to communicate "quality AND price AND tradition AND new flavor" in one post dilutes all of them.

05
Principle
The algorithm rewards what humans already reward.

Platform fluency isn't optional anymore

Working inside Meta's content evaluation frameworks taught me this: the algorithm rewards what humans already reward. Write for the person scrolling at 11pm, not for the brand guidelines doc.
